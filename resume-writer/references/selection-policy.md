# Resume selection policy

Use this policy after building a target-program matching judgment and before drafting. It is a lexicographic decision rule, not a single relevance score:

```text
experience family priority → evidence strength → target fit → personal contribution → distinctiveness → space cost
```

## Experience family priority

Use this default ordering when deciding which sections and entries survive a one-page limit:

1. Internship / Work Experience
2. Competition Experience, especially verified prize-bearing work
3. Research Experience
4. Project Experience, including ordinary course projects

This is a priority, not an automatic override. First assess target-program fit and the capability gap each item fills. A weakly evidenced competition entry may be displaced by a substantially stronger, highly matched research or project entry; conversely, a strong award-bearing competition entry may displace a more relevant but ordinary course project. Select the combination that best fills one page; sections are optional. Record the matching rationale and decision in the evidence matrix. Competition awards must be shown in `COMPETITION EXPERIENCE`, not hidden only in `SKILLS`.

Every selected Competition Experience entry must name its verified award level/result in the entry itself. An entry without a confirmed prize or result is not eligible for the competition family until the fact is confirmed.

## Target matching judgment

For each candidate, record:

- the target-program capability or technical area it supports;
- direct, adjacent, or weak match strength;
- evidence strength, personal contribution, and outcome intensity;
- whether it adds a distinct signal not already covered by a stronger item;
- estimated space cost and whether it should be selected, compressed, or omitted.

Do this matching pass before choosing section membership. A project with a direct match can be retained over a weak competition entry, but an award-bearing competition entry with clear contribution normally outranks an ordinary course project.

## Default tiers

| Tier | Evidence family | Default priority |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Verified internship or employment with concrete duties | Highest |
| 2 | Verified national/provincial award, competition result, scholarship, or award-bearing competition project | High |
| 3 | Research, innovation, software copyright, patent, applied system, or substantial independent project | Medium-high |
| 4 | Strong course project with a complete method and result | Medium |
| 5 | Ordinary exercise, incomplete item, or title-only project | Lowest |

Professional relevance can decide between items in the same tier. It can also justify retaining a lower-tier item when a higher-tier item has weak evidence or no usable contribution, but it does not automatically overturn a well-supported higher-tier item. For a one-page resume, retain the strongest bullets from the highest useful tiers rather than giving every experience equal space.

## Evidence strength

Prefer items with, in order:

1. a traceable Markdown source and confirmed dates/title;
2. a clear individual action or responsibility;
3. a specific method, tool, deliverable, or result;
4. a defensible metric, score, ranking, award, publication, software copyright, or deployment outcome;
5. wording that can survive an interview follow-up.

Do not convert team outcomes into personal achievements. If ownership is unclear, ask for confirmation or use a bounded verb such as `contributed to` only when contribution itself is confirmed.

## Target adaptation

For each target program, explain why each selected item is useful. A target-specific resume must change at least three of evidence order, selected experiences, bullet emphasis, course selection, skills grouping, or capability signal. Keep high-tier experience visible even when the target is specialized; compress it before removing it, unless the facts are incomplete or the target explicitly requires a different format.
